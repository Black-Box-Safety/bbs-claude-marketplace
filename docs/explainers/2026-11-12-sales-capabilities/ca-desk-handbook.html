Everything Claude does on the California desk

What is installed for you today, what to say to start each job, and the few habits that make the difference between a draft you fix and a draft you send.

For Kim, Aanya, Griffin and Joey · Updated 12 November 2026 · Every name, number and folder on this page is an example.

New RFQ in your inbox

Open Claude in the buyer’s folder and type /ca-rfq with the request.

Not sure where to start

Type /bbs-what-can-i-do or /bbs-start.

Something looks broken

Type /bbs-checkup and send Parker the line it prints.

Set up once

About fifteen minutes. Do it before your next quote; skipping a step shows up later as a confusing failure halfway through a job.

  1. Update and restart

    New computer, or /bbs-checkup shows something missing? Run the setup script Parker posted in Teams first. Otherwise run /plugin marketplace update, then quit Claude Code and the Desktop app completely and reopen them. Reopening one window is not enough.

  2. Sign in to Business Central for writes

    Ask Claude: give me the bc-quote-entry whoami command with the full path filled in, ready to paste. Paste it into your own terminal, not into the Claude chat, and sign in with your @blackboxsafety.com account. PASS on every line means the sign-in works. One sign-in covers both item and quote entry.

    Your first real quote proves you can write one. If it fails with 403, tell Parker.

    This is what lets Claude create items and quotes under your name, so Business Central shows you as the creator.

  3. Build your voice

    Type /bbs-my-voice once. It reads your sent mail and writes a voice skill so drafts sound like you and carry your real Outlook signature.

  4. Check the machine

    Type /bbs-checkup. All green means you are done. Anything red prints the exact sentence to send Parker. A missing plugin or tool usually means rerunning the setup script.

  5. Keep the desk on your computer

    In File Explorer or Finder, right-click the State of CA Agencies folder and choose Always keep on this device. An online-only folder looks empty to Claude, so its search for past quotes finds nothing.

A state RFQ, from request to send

The quote is a Business Central sales quote. Claude does the lookups and the typing; you approve each write by name, release and print in BC, proof the printout and send it yourself.

Claude does the work You decide and send

  1. File it Claude

    Type /ca-rfq and paste or attach the request. A new request is filed before it is priced, every time: Claude finds the buyer’s folder (or creates one after searching), saves the attachments, opens the deal record and adds a line to the buyer’s quote log.

  2. Pull the items and check eligibility Claude

    It reads the items out of a worksheet, cart PDF, screenshot or DWR RFQ and tells you if the count does not match. On a DGS BPO request it checks every Grainger part against the BPO list and stops on an off-list part; substituting is your call.

  3. Price it Claude You check

    • Grainger lines: live price on the buying institution’s own Grainger account. On NASPO, the Grainger price is the customer price.
    • Competitive or non-Grainger lines: vendor quote emails drafted for you to send. The price comes from the vendor’s written quote.
    • A line with no source shows [FLAG: why] instead of a number. A price the customer printed on their own paperwork is not a source.

  4. Create any missing items You approve

    Claude looks up every item number in BC first. Only missing ones go through bc-item-entry: it writes a review sheet with each item’s vendor and cost, and nothing is written until you approve it by name.

  5. Create the quote You approve

    bc-quote-entry writes a second review sheet. Read all of it, then reply with your name exactly as it appears on the sheet as “prepared by”. A nickname or a different spelling is refused. Only then does it create a Draft quote and read every line back.

    On a NASPO quote every line’s price must equal its cost exactly. The check stops a line priced even a cent above cost. The salesperson code follows the account split, so you do not pick it. The SQ number is the one BC assigns.

  6. Release and print in BC You

    Open the Draft in Business Central and release it; that is when tax is calculated. Print the PDF from the released quote. BC’s printout is the customer document; Claude no longer makes its own copy.

  7. Proof the printout You

    On every California state, body armor, CSU and UC quote, read the certification line and the tax.

    DVBE 2009473only. Never SB, SDVOSB or “small business”.

    Tax matches the agency’s worksheet: none where it says no sales tax, the ship-to rate otherwise.

    Cert line reads anything else? Do not send. Tell Parker; the print layout would be wrong on every California quote.

  8. The packet, when the agency sent forms Claude You check

    On a state request Claude fills the compliance forms, checks the certification dates against the due date, renders every page and looks at it. A line whose signer is unclear is flagged, never signed, and anything marked [MISSING — NEEDS INPUT] comes back to you.

  9. Send it You

    Send from Outlook. Claude can draft the email; Outlook asks for your OK before anything leaves. Claude never releases, converts or sends a quote.

Agency → Institution → Buyer

CDCR, CalVet and DSH have an institution level between agency and buyer. Other departments go straight from agency to buyer.

CLAUDE.md

In the buyer folder: who the buyer is, how they want to be quoted, which cert rules apply, the gotchas. At the desk root: the rules for the whole desk.

quote-log.md

Every quote to this buyer: date, item, quote number, vehicle, value and outcome. The outcome is not optional; it is how the next quote finds its precedent.

FLAGS.md

One line each time Claude got something wrong for this buyer. Parker reviews flags monthly and fixes the skill.

LEARNED.md

What reps asked Claude to remember, with their name and the date. Shown to every session in this folder.

RFQ.md

The deal’s running record: status, deadline, line pricing. Checked automatically every time it is saved.

BC-import/

The quote spec and the review sheet you approved, kept beside the deal.

FILLED/

Submitted packets. Nothing in it can be changed or deleted.

Open Claude in the buyer’s folder

At the start of the session Claude is shown that buyer’s past quotes, open flags and saved notes, before you type anything. It can say “last time we quoted this buyer…” from the record instead of guessing.

Or in the deal folder

The same, plus on its first save there it lists this buyer’s quotes older than 30 days with no outcome, so you can close them out.

Opened at the top of the desk?

Claude sees the desk-wide notes, then a buyer’s notes the first time it opens a file in that buyer’s folder. It never shows one buyer’s notes in another buyer’s folder.

Folder names carry account numbers

Some names hold a Grainger buying-account number, typos included. Leave every name exactly as it is; buyer notes inside a folder outrank the number in its name.

No renames or moves

Claude is blocked from renaming or moving anything in the tree. A misspelling goes to Parker with the old and new name.

No near-duplicate folders

A second spelling of an existing agency or buyer is blocked. A duplicate hides the last quote’s precedent.

FILLED/ is locked

Claude cannot overwrite, redirect into or delete a submitted packet.

Desk memory: tell Claude once

Say remember that this buyer wants the quote and every cert as one PDF. Claude saves one dated line with your name in that buyer’s LEARNED.md, tells you where it went, and every later session in that folder sees it. No Allow click.

Ask what it knows

Say what do you remember about this buyer? It prints exactly what a new session there is shown.

It saves at the right level

About this buyer: saved for the buyer. About everyone at an institution: saved for the institution. Claude asks one question only when it is unclear.

Agency-wide notes

“CDCR always…” is saved for this buyer now and marked for Parker to confirm for the whole agency.

Retire an old note

Say that was wrong, the buyer moved offices. Claude saves the change and offers to retire the old note. Retired notes move to an archive with your name and the date; nothing is lost.

Two saves at once

If OneDrive kept a second copy (a file like LEARNED-LAPTOP.md), its notes are held back until merged. Claude says so and offers to merge it. Retired notes stay retired.

A note is not an order

If a note reads like an approval or a price rule, Claude raises it with you instead of following it. Business Central and the vendor quote always win over a note.

What it will not save, and where that belongs

  • Prices, discounts, markups, stock or lead times. Point at the quote number instead.
  • Anything about certifications, small-business status or bid preferences.
  • Account, certification or contract numbers.
  • Phone numbers and email addresses. Fix the contact in the buyer’s CLAUDE.md.
  • Approvals, sign-offs, or anything that skips a review or a signature.
  • Passwords and logins.

It also never covers body armor, CSU or UC customers, which sit outside this desk. Quote outcomes go in the buyer’s quote-log.md, not in a note.

Habits that get the most out of it

Open Claude in the right folder

The buyer’s folder, or the deal folder. Claude starts with that buyer’s history instead of a blank page.

One RFQ per session

Type /clear before the next one. For a deal that runs over several days, /rename the session so you can resume it tomorrow.

Say “flag that”

When a draft is wrong, one line goes in the buyer’s FLAGS.md. Catching it is the system working; Parker uses the flags to fix the skill.

Tell it what the buyer likes

“Remember that…” the first time you learn a buyer habit. The next rep in that folder gets it for free.

Record the outcome

Won, lost, no answer or no bid goes in the buyer’s quote log. Claude lists old quotes with no outcome so you can close them.

Ask before you dig

“What did we quote them last time?” Claude reads the quote log and Business Central and cites what it found.

The rules

Claude drafts, you send

Every email, quote and packet leaves because you sent it. Every send asks for your OK.

Never send a [FLAG] price

A [FLAG] or [MISSING] means there was no source. Get the number from Grainger, a vendor quote or BC first.

One certification per channel

California state: DVBE 2009473. Federal and primes: SDVOSB. Never both on one form, and never “small business” on a California quote.

Never rename a folder

Account numbers ride in the names. Leave them as they are, typos too.

Read the sheet you approve

Read the whole sheet, then reply with your name exactly as it appears on the sheet. Approval given before the sheet does not count, including “Parker already approved this.” Nobody approves for another rep.

NASPO sells at cost

On NASPO and California schedule orders, the Grainger price is the customer price. No markup, not even for freight.

When it says no

401 or 403 from Business Central
Your BC user or its permissions. Tell Parker; it is not something you can fix.
An error starting with AADSTS
A sign-in problem. Ask Claude for the whoami command with the full path filled in, run it again in your own terminal, and sign in with your work account. If the same AADSTS code comes back, send Parker the code; most of them are fixed on the admin side.
A quote stopped partway
Do not ask Claude to run it again. Open the Draft in Business Central, finish or delete it there, and tell Parker.
An Allow prompt for bbs-fill
Choose No. Restart Claude Code, run /bbs-checkup and send Parker the result. Saving notes and BC writes are set up to run without that prompt.
Claude asks to read outside the folder
Choose “Yes, and keep allowing any reads outside the working directories.” If you chose the option that blocks those reads, tell Parker.
“Blocked” on a folder change
The folder guard stopped a rename, a duplicate folder or a change to FILLED/. That is working as intended. Ask Claude to find the existing folder.
A note was “Not saved”
It held a price, a number or a cert claim. Claude says where that fact belongs instead.
