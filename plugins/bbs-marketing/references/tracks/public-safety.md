# Track: Public Safety

Body armor, duty gear, and tactical equipment for California law enforcement — the agency
(equipment unit, quartermaster, chief) and, downstream, the individual officer buying
personal gear. Shared identity, credentials, and company facts live in `references/core.md`;
this file is only what makes this expression different from `tracks/mro-procurement.md`.
Scope tags for `brand-check`: `--scope public-safety`, plus `--scope agency` on anything
addressed to a department or its personnel.

## Read the source brochure for structure, never for copy

`sources/BBS_Body_Tactical_Brochure_250306_R1.pdf` is this track's reference piece and it
carries four known errors, all now governed by `references/CLAIMS.md`: a transposed body armor
contract number on p2 (p1 is the correct rendering), a headquarters city that names the
county instead of the city, a contact email that no longer routes, and a certification
rendering that credits the wrong agency. Take the argument order and the roster from that
PDF; take every word of a factual statement from the registry. Same rule for
`sources/LE and MRO Brochure Digital.pdf` — read it to see how the two tracks sit side by
side, not to source copy.

## The wedge: specialist credibility

Order of argument on every agency-facing piece:

1. **We supply this category specifically** — armor, plates, carriers, duty gear.
2. **Named specialist brands**, not "leading manufacturers" (roster below).
3. **Contract-ready access** — the vehicle exists, the pricing is already competed.

Never open a public-safety piece on procurement mechanics, DVBE goal attainment, or spend
credit. Those belong to the MRO track; used here they make BBS sound like a broker rather
than an equipment supplier. Compliance value, if it appears at all, is a closing note.

| Accent | Gold rules and keylines on black | Grainger red plus navy |
| Display type | Condensed, all-caps, tight tracking | Sentence-case, regular weight |
| Imagery | Golden-hour photography of working officers in issued kit | Facility, warehouse, PPE product |
| Co-brand | None — BBS stands alone | Grainger Distributor Alliance lockup |

- Charcoal/black grounds carry the piece; white is a content field inside them, not the base.
- Gold is a rule, a keyline, or a section head — never a fill behind body copy, never a
  headline color on white.
- Headings set condensed all-caps; body copy stays in the core text face at normal weight.
- Photography shows real officers in issued equipment at working distance, daylight or golden
  hour. No studio-lit models, no muzzle-forward hero poses, no flag overlays, no
  desaturated-with-one-red-accent treatments.
- **Never place the Grainger lockup on a public-safety piece.** That co-brand is MRO-only and
  here it reads as a facilities vendor selling armor.

## Voice register

Direct, specialist, credible to someone who wears the equipment. Assume a reader who can name
every brand in the roster and who will proofread.

- Say the model, the level, the fit, the lead time. Specifics do the persuading.
- No tactical cosplay: no warrior/sheepdog/thin-line framing, no "battle-tested," no
  "operator," no manufactured mission language.
- **Never use "End of Watch"** in any commercial copy, headline, product name, or campaign
  name. It means a line-of-duty death.
- **Never send urgency copy into a backorder.** Long-lead items read "made to order — reserves
  ship in order received," never "order now."
- No exclamation points, no hype adjectives. One adjective per section, and only if it carries
  information. If a sentence would survive being read aloud in a briefing room, it stays.

## Buyers

- **Equipment unit** — accountable for fit, certification, and issue records. Moved by sizing
  and fit coverage, lead-time honesty, warranty handling. Give them spec grids, not benefits.
- **Quartermaster** — accountable for stock, reorders, and the paperwork behind each issue.
  Moved by one reorder path and pricing that needs no re-competing. Give them the vehicle
  number and a reorder contact.
- **Chief / command staff** — accountable for officer safety, budget defensibility, audit
  exposure. Moved by proof the buy was competed and by named agencies already served. Give
  them one paragraph and the contract vehicle.
- **Individual officer** — spending their own money or an allowance. Moved by "we supply your
  department's gear," plain specs, real availability, class-wide officer pricing. Give them
  the product, the price, and the lead time.

## Compliance surface

1. **Never imply agency sponsorship, affiliation, or endorsement.** No department name in a
   headline position; no agency seal, patch, badge, or insignia used as a mark. CA Vehicle
   Code § 2262 restricts "CHP" usage suggesting association. Agency names appear only as
   factual statements of who we supply.
2. **The `agency-disclaimer` wording is required verbatim** on every agency-targeted piece and
   every officer-facing piece that names an agency. It is `presence: required` for scope
   `agency` and was absent from both shipped print pieces — check it explicitly.
3. **FPPC ethics.** Discounts stay modest and class-wide (all officers), framed as "officer
   pricing." Never an individual, enhanced, or targeted offer to procurement or equipment-unit
   personnel, and never gear, samples, or pricing to the person who decides the award.
4. **Vendor MAP.** Confirm manufacturer authorization before showing a discount. Show MSRP;
   put discounts behind codes.
5. **CAN-SPAM** on officer-facing email: unsubscribe link and physical address in every send,
   and confirmed marketing consent — an agency-program buyer is a transactional customer, not
   a subscriber.
6. Run `brand-check` with `--scope public-safety --scope agency` before any render. Badge and
   logo artwork is invisible to the gate, so read contract numbers set as artwork by eye.

## Piece types

- **Sell sheet** (1 page, agency) — one category, the vehicle, that category's brands, a spec
  grid, disclaimer, contact.
- **Line card** (1–2 pages, agency) — the full roster table plus the three governed contract
  numbers. The workhorse leave-behind for an equipment-unit visit.
- **Capability one-pager** (1 page, command staff) — what we supply, which vehicles, who we
  already supply, credentials from `core.md`. Contract numbers, no procurement theory.
- **Officer-facing email** (retail) — one product or bundle, plain specs, honest lead time,
  class-wide officer pricing behind a code, unsubscribe and address, disclaimer when an agency
  is named.
