# Voice

How Black Box Safety writes. One voice, two registers. Draft against the register, then check
every factual assertion against `references/CLAIMS.md` before it renders. If a fact you need is not
registered, write `NEEDS CLAIM: <description>` in the draft. Never invent the wording.

## How BBS writes

The shared register: warm, plain, efficient. Confident without hype. Talk to a busy
professional like a helpful peer, not a marketer. The Branding Guide describes the brand as
strong, dependable, confident, clean, and compact. Write the same way.

- Short, direct sentences. Vary the rhythm. Do not drone at one length.
- Active voice. Black Box Safety is the subject of the verb that serves the customer, always.
- Concrete nouns and verbs over abstractions. "Switch your punchout," not "leverage our
  procurement solution."
- Specific beats clever. A contract number, a lead time, a model number. To both audiences,
  specificity reads as credibility and adjectives read as filler. One adjective per section,
  and only if it carries information a number cannot.
- Name the zeros out loud: no added cost, no new bid, no new login, no workflow change.
- Legal name is **Black Box Safety, Inc.** on first reference, *Black Box Safety* after. *BBS*
  is internal shorthand and never appears customer-facing.
- One clear ask per piece. Offer more than one channel if you must, but only one action.

## Per-track register

**The one-sentence tell.** Ask what the line is accountable to. Accountable to an audit, a
contract file, or a budget line, it is MRO, and it would survive being read aloud in a
procurement review. Accountable to a piece of equipment on a person, it is public safety, and
it would survive being read aloud in a briefing room. A line that would sound at home in a
recruiting video belongs on neither.

**MRO / procurement: calm, procedural, procurement-literate.** Write the way a good contract
analyst talks. Paperwork-forward. Lead with compliance for the procurement officer and with
"nothing changes" for the facilities manager and the requisitioner. Say the vehicle number,
the mechanism, and the cost delta. Never let Grainger be the subject of the verb that serves
the customer: Black Box Safety delivers, backed by Grainger's national distribution network.

**Public safety: direct, specialist, credible to someone who wears the equipment.** Assume a
reader who can name every brand in the roster and who will proofread. Say the model, the
level, the fit, and the lead time, and let the specifics persuade. Never open on procurement
mechanics or DVBE spend credit. Here that makes Black Box Safety read as a broker.

## Law-enforcement culture rules

These apply to the public-safety track and to any officer-facing piece on any track.

1. **No tactical cosplay.** No warrior, sheepdog, or thin-line framing. No "battle-tested,"
   no "operator," no manufactured mission language, no flag overlays in copy or art direction.
2. **Never use "End of Watch"** in any commercial copy, headline, product name, or campaign
   name. It means a line-of-duty death.
3. **Never send urgency copy into a backorder.** Long-lead items read "made to order. Reserves
   ship in order received." Never "order now" or "limited stock."
4. **Discounts stay modest and class-wide**, framed as "officer pricing." Never an individual
   or enhanced offer, and never to the person who decides an award.
5. **Never imply agency sponsorship or endorsement.** No department name in a headline
   position, no seal or patch used as a mark. Agency names appear only as factual statements
   of who we supply, and the `agency-disclaimer` wording is then required verbatim.
6. **Proofread twice.** This audience finds the typo, and it costs more than a weak headline.

## Anti-AI-tell rules

- **No em dashes or en dashes.** Use a period, comma, colon, or parentheses. This is the
  single most reliable AI tell. Treat it as a hard constraint.
- **No throat-clearing or hype clusters.** No "in today's fast-paced landscape," no "we're
  proud to," no opener that warms up before it says anything. Start on the claim.
- **No rule-of-three padding.** Do not force every idea into a trio.
- **No promotional inflation.** Do not say it is groundbreaking. Show the specific thing it does.
- **No vague attributions.** Not "experts agree." Name the agency, cite the contract.
- **Watch boldface.** Bold the few phrases that carry the scan, not every other word.
- **Sentence case in headings**, not Title Case On Every Word.
- **No generic upbeat closers.** End on the action or a concrete fact.

## Banned words and phrases

Cut on sight, both tracks: seamless, robust, unlock, elevate, empower, cutting-edge,
state-of-the-art, best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, game-changing, groundbreaking,
innovative, vibrant, boasts, delve, navigate the complexities, holistic, synergy, ecosystem,
one-stop shop, turnkey, mission-driven, solutions (standing alone as a noun), leverage (as a
verb), streamline, revolutionize, transform, extreme customer service, enjoyable, exciting,
rest assured, peace of mind, at the end of the day, we're excited to, we're proud to, let us
help you, trusted partner, and more!, experts agree, in today's landscape.

Public safety adds: warrior, sheepdog, thin blue line, operator, battle-tested, tip of the
spear, hero, End of Watch, limited stock, order now, act fast. MRO adds any construction where
Grainger serves the customer: "Grainger handles it," "Grainger ships and we collect," "we just
process the paperwork," "pass-through."

## Headlines, subheads, and CTAs

**Headlines.** Sentence case. One claim, no subordinate clause. Eight words is the ceiling,
five is better. State the outcome for the reader, not a fact about us. MRO headlines resolve
to a change in status ("Same Grainger. Same prices. Now it counts."). Public-safety headlines
resolve to a category and a buyer ("Body armor for California law enforcement"). No agency
name in a headline position. No questions, no puns, no exclamation points.

**Subheads.** One sentence that adds the mechanism the headline asserted rather than restating
it. If it could be deleted without losing information, delete it. Eyebrows are uppercase, two
or three words, and label the section rather than sell it ("Contract info," not "Why it
matters").

**CTAs.** One ask per piece, in the imperative, naming the action and the channel together:
"Scan to open the punchout," "Request contract pricing," "Email joey@blackboxsafety.com for
the line card." Never "learn more," "get started," or "contact us today." On officer email the
CTA sits above the unsubscribe link and the physical address, both required.

NEEDS CLAIM: the DVBE participation goal percentage (MRO wedge headline); NIJ certification
wording (public-safety headlines and spec copy); public phone number and web address (every
contact block and CTA); catalog scale figures, meaning product and manufacturer counts (MRO
headlines); named peer-agency precedent (the strongest proof line the MRO track can carry).
