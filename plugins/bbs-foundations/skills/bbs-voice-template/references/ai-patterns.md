# The 35 AI-Writing Patterns

Adapted from [blader/humanizer](https://github.com/blader/humanizer) (MIT License), which
derives the catalog from Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing." Work through these on any draft
before applying a voice profile. Notes in *italics* are BBS-wide; a person's own profile
adds their specific tells on top.

## Content patterns (1-6)

1. **Inflated claims about importance and legacy** — Remove "pivotal moment," "marks a shift," "indelible mark." *A quote is a quote, not a partnership milestone.*
2. **Name-dropping to prove importance** — Cut lists that prove nothing without context. *Contract vehicles belong in the signature block, not sprinkled through the body.*
3. **Shallow analysis with -ing phrases** — Replace "symbolizing X, reflecting Y" with direct statements of what something does.
4. **Sales language** — Remove promotional words when describing things factually. *State the spec, not the virtue.*
5. **Vague sources** — Replace "Experts argue" with a named source or drop the claim. *"The manufacturer indicated" needs a name and a date.*
6. **Formulaic challenges and outlook sections** — Cut stock "Despite these challenges" or "Future prospects" unless they carry facts and dates.

## Language patterns (7-13)

7. **Overused AI words** — Reduce "crucial," "showcase," "landscape," "testament," "underscore," "pivotal." *BBS ban list: leverage, robust, seamless, best-in-class, value-add, streamline, empower, elevate.*
8. **Avoiding is and are** — Replace "serves as," "features," "boasts" with "is," "has," "contains."
9. **Not X but Y and clipped negative endings** — Rewrite "It's not just X, it's Y" and "no guessing" into complete clauses.
10. **Forced groups of three** — Break artificial triads into natural pairs or separate thoughts.
11. **Changing names and repeating sentence openings** — One consistent name; vary consecutive sentence subjects.
12. **False from X to Y ranges** — Remove "from X to Y" when X and Y are not a spectrum.
13. **Passive voice and missing subjects** — "I quoted this," never "this was quoted."

## Style patterns (14-19)

14. **Em and en dashes** — Replace with periods, commas, colons, or parentheses unless the person's own sample uses them regularly. *Check the sample; several BBS writers never use one.*
15. **Too much bold text** — Remove bold unless it is a heading or serves a clear organizational job. *Most BBS email has no bold in the body at all.*
16. **Lists with bold mini-headings** — Convert "**Label:** text" bullets into prose or plain numbered items.
17. **Title case in headings** — Standard headline capitalization, not every word.
18. **Emojis** — Remove decorative emojis.
19. **Curly quotation marks** — Match the target format.

## Chatbot patterns (20-35)

20. **Chatbot text left in the answer** — Remove "I hope this helps," "Would you like me to expand?" *The tell is a chatbot offering to do more work. A salesperson inviting a reply ("Please let me know if you have any questions") may be the person's real closer — check their profile before cutting it.*
21. **Knowledge-limit disclaimers and guesses** — Cut "as of my last update" and speculative gap-filling. *Never guess a lead time, price, or part number. Bracket it and flag it.*
22. **Overly agreeable tone** — No "Great question!" before the answer.
23. **Filler phrases** — "in order to," "due to the fact that," "at this point in time" → direct equivalents.
24. **Too many qualifiers** — Keep necessary hedging; remove stacked qualifiers.
25. **Generic positive endings** — Cut vague optimism. *End on the ask.*
26. **Too many hyphenated word pairs** — Hyphen before a noun, not after.
27. **Pretending to reveal a deeper truth** — Replace "The real question is" with the point.
28. **Announcing the next point** — Remove "Let's dive in," "Here's what you need to know." *A person's own real transition into a list ("Some quick notes:") is voice, not preamble — check the profile.*
29. **A heading repeated in the first sentence** — Delete it.
30. **Writing about the previous version** — Reference a prior quote only when the history matters to the decision.
31. **Forced punchlines and dramatic fragments** — Natural rhythm, not rows of short emphatic sentences.
32. **Formulaic sayings** — Replace "X is the Y of Z" with what actually happens.
33. **Fake-candid openings** — Remove "Honestly?" and "Let's be honest."
34. **Answering objections no one raised** — Cut "I'm not arguing that..." unless a real counter-argument is named.
35. **Rejecting fake alternatives** — Remove strawman options the text rejects and never uses.
