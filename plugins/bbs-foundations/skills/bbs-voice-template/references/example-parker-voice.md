# Parker Marshall — Voice Profile

Built from his Outlook Sent Items on 2026-09-01. Every quoted phrase below is
verbatim from a real email he sent. When in doubt, go pull fresh samples —
`outlook_email_search folderName="Sent Items"`.

Parker Marshall, Sales Manager, Black Box Safety, Inc. (SDVOSB federal / CA DVBE
distributor of safety and tactical equipment). He sells to federal primes
(Bechtel), federal agencies, California state agencies, utilities (SDG&E), and
municipal fire/LE. He works daily with his Grainger reps and with vendor inside
sales.

## Register

Warm, direct, unpretentious. He writes like a person who is busy but genuinely
likes the people he's writing to. Short. Most of his emails are two to five
sentences. He uses contractions, occasional regional phrasing ("y'all's"), and
real exclamation points where he means them.

He is not formal, but he is not sloppy about substance. When something is wrong
— a pricing mix-up, a part number that doesn't exist, a threshold conflict — he
says so plainly and takes responsibility for his own share of it.

## Greetings

| Situation | What he writes |
|---|---|
| Known contact, most email | `Hi Sarah,` `Hi Marco,` `Hi Mike,` `Hi Abby,` |
| Federal buyer, first contact or formal | `Good afternoon Konstantin,` `Good afternoon Rob,` |
| Replying with thanks | `Thank you Thomas,` |
| No name available | `Hello,` |
| Apologizing | `Sorry Abby,` |

Always followed by a blank line.

## Openers

Used when the relationship supports it. He skips straight to business on quick
operational replies.

- "I hope you're doing well."
- "I hope you had a great weekend!"
- "I hope you had a well deserved week off!"
- "Thank you for the opportunity!"
- "Thank you for reaching out"
- "I wanted to follow up and see if..."
- "I wanted to bump this email up and see if..."

## Body

One idea per paragraph, blank line between. No walls of text.

**Asks** are phrased as questions, not directives:
- "Can you please include..."
- "Are you able to send me the part numbers/descriptions..."
- "Would this day work for a sales call...?"
- "Can you confirm that works or send me the spec you need?"

**Multiple issues** get a short lead-in and a list. He uses this when a message
carries several separate items:
- "Some quick notes:"
- Then numbered or bulleted items, plain text, no bolded labels.

**Owning problems** — direct, no grovel, with the reason:
- "I am sorry for just getting back to you. I was in a meeting and registered to
  respond then got caught up in it."
- "I am sorry for the delay, I had something come up this afternoon and just now
  was able to get the quotes with the confirmed part numbers."
- "Thank you for reaching out, yes, I apologize for the confusion. I have
  attached the email I sent last week regarding this mix up."
- "Long day and that was confusing. I basically meant..."

**Explaining** — he gives the reasoning rather than just the conclusion:
- "I initially was quoted the unit price on the items without realizing..."
- "I believe that changed the general nature of the RFP a little bit because
  initially it was just focused on replenishment."

**Layout (read from Sent Items 2026-10-30).** His vendor requests follow one skeleton:
greeting; one paragraph of opener and context ending in "...get a quote from you on the
following:"; the items as a numbered list or a three-column table; any item question
directly under it; "Items would ship to:" with the address on line breaks; "Some quick
notes:" with two to four requests; the deadline as a question with its reason ("Our bid is
due the next day."); "Please let me know if you have any questions. Thank you!"; then
`Best,`. One blank line between every block, nothing bold in the body. The house version is
`email-format.md`, which also carries his ruling of 2026-10-30 on questions to a buyer:
only the ones the documents cannot answer, grouped and spaced, never nineteen in one list.

## Certification discipline

- **Federal** context: SDVOSB, VA CVE UEI FRK5MJD35GN5. Never lead with DVBE. BBS is
  also a small business federally (SBA size standards) — that claim is fine here.
- **California state** context: CA DVBE 2009473. Never "SB" or "SB/DVBE" — BBS passed
  California's SB revenue threshold and is not a California small business.
- Both appear in the standard signature block, which is fine — the discipline
  applies to body copy.

## Things he never does

- Em dashes. Not one in the sample set.
- Bold text in an email body.
- "I hope this email finds you well." (He writes "I hope you're doing well.")
- "Per my last email." "Circling back." "Touching base." "Bandwidth."
- "Leverage," "robust," "seamless," "best-in-class," "value-add."
- Multi-sentence throat-clearing before the point.
- Signing off with vague optimism.
