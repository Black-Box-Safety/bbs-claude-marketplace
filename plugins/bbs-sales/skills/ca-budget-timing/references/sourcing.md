# Sourcing a date so a rep can defend it

A date a rep cannot source is a date they will abandon the moment a buyer questions it. Every
figure and deadline that leaves this skill carries where it came from.

## Where the authorities live

| Source | Covers | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **The enacted Budget Act** (the budget bill as chaptered, plus its trailer bills) | Every appropriation, by item number | The authoritative amount, fiscal year, and any item-stated encumbrance deadline |
| **Legislative Analyst's Office**, lao.ca.gov/budget | Analysis of the Governor's Budget, the May Revision and the enacted budget, by department | Understanding what an augmentation is *for*, and whether it is one-time. Analysis, not authority — cite the Budget Act for the number |
| **DGS bulletins** (Purchasing Authority / procurement bulletins) | Purchasing authority thresholds, year-end cutoff dates | The ~1 April DGS cutoff. This is the one that moves; always cite the current fiscal year's bulletin by number |
| **State Contracting Manual**, Vol. 2 | The SB/DVBE Option mechanics | §1200 for the threshold, the two-quote rule, and the no-advertising provision. Cite the revision date |
| **Gov. Code §16304 / SAM §8325** | Encumbrance and reversion | The PO-not-delivery rule |
| **The budget corpus** in `Customers/State of CA Agencies/.sales-team/` | The above, digested per agency | Fast lookup. Always check `as_of`; it is derived, not authoritative |

## How to cite

In a rep-facing output, the citation is one parenthetical, not a footnote apparatus:

> "Encumbrance means a signed PO by 30 June, not a delivery (Gov. Code §16304, SAM §8325)."

> "Their facilities item runs to 30 September — federal fund source (Budget Act of <year>,
> item <number>; corpus as of <date>)."

Two rules about citations:

- **Cite the primary source, not the digest.** The corpus is where you looked it up; the
  Budget Act item is where it is true.
- **When the source is a bulletin pattern rather than a statute, say so.** "Pattern from DGS
  Bulletin <number>; verify the current-year bulletin before quoting this to a buyer" is an
  honest sentence and it prevents a confident wrong answer.

If a date cannot be sourced this run, it does not go in front of a buyer. Data pending.
