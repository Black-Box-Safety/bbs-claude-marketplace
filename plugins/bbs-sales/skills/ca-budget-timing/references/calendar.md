# The calendar, and how to read the current corpus

## The dates that do not move

| Date | Authority | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ~10 January | Cal. Const. Art. IV §12(a) | Governor's Budget must be submitted by 10 January |
| ~14 May | Gov. Code §13308(e) | May Revision |
| 15 June | Cal. Const. Art. IV §12(c) | Legislature must pass the budget bill |
| 30 June | End of fiscal year | Encumbrance deadline for appropriations lapsing that year |
| 1 July | Start of fiscal year | California fiscal years run July–June. FY26-27 means Jul 2026 – Jun 2027 |
| ~1 April | DGS bulletin pattern, non-IT goods | Practical cutoff for purchases going through DGS. **Not statutory — verify the current bulletin** |
| 30 September | Federal fiscal year end | Governs federally funded items only. Check the item |

## Reading the corpus's encumbrance table

The budget corpus carries, per appropriation, roughly: agency, item number, amount, fiscal
year, one-time or ongoing, encumbrance deadline, and the fund source. The reading order that
avoids the usual mistakes:

1. **Fund source first.** Federal fund source means the deadline is probably not 30 June.
2. **One-time or ongoing.** A one-time item is a window. Note its closing date and work
   backwards from it, not from the fiscal year end.
3. **Encumbrance deadline as stated in the item**, not inferred from the fiscal year. Where
   the item states a deadline, that deadline wins over any general rule.
4. **Contingency flags.** An item conditioned on other legislation is not spendable money and
   must be reported as contingent, with what it is contingent on.
5. **`as_of`.** Print it beside every figure taken from the table.

## When the corpus and a buyer disagree

The buyer wins on their own timeline, and you should ask rather than assert. The corpus tells
you which question to ask — "your facilities item runs to September rather than June, does
that match what you're working to?" — which is a better call than either guessing or
lecturing.

Record the answer. A buyer-confirmed deadline is better evidence than the corpus and belongs
in the account record with the date and the buyer's name.
