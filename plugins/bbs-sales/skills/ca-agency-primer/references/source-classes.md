# Keeping public and internal sources apart

An agency primer mixes two kinds of truth that look identical on the page and are not. The
whole discipline of this document is keeping them visibly separate.

## Public — the agency's own documents

What the department publishes about itself: mission, facility list, per-site population and
capacity, staffing, program mix, enacted budget, capacity projects.

**Prefer the budget estimate documents over the marketing pages.** A department's May
Revision or Governor's Budget estimate carries per-facility operational detail — census,
licensed capacity, staffing, program changes, project status — that a facility's public page
does not. It is also the agency's own number, which means a rep can reference it without
risk.

Cite the document and its date. "As of the July 1 actual" or "2026-27 Governor's Budget,
Item 4440" is the difference between a fact and a claim.

Safe to reference with a customer. It is their own publication.

## Hard internal — Business Central and the CRM

Revenue, invoices, AOV, cadence, AR, categories, vehicle mix, pipeline, contacts.

Always carry the as-of date and the scope statement. Cancelled invoices excluded.

Facts about the customer's own orders can be discussed with that customer. Facts about
another facility cannot.

## SCPRS-derived — slice-limited

Competitor prices, category sizing, share-shaped figures. Internal posture only. See
`../ca-site-visit-brief/references/confidence-ledger.md` for why, and for the two ways it
goes wrong in a room.

## Data pending

A facility with no Business Central history is a cold start. Write "data pending" and say
what would have to be true to know. Do not write zero, do not interpolate from a sibling
site, do not put a target on it.

## The sentence-level rule

Never let two classes share a sentence unlabeled.

- Wrong: "Patton has 1,329 patients and does $40K with us — huge opportunity."
- Right: "Patton's census is 1,329 (DSH May Revision, July 1 actual). BBS revenue there was
  $39,953 in FY25-26 (BC, as of 2026-09-03). A sibling site of comparable size did $1.2M."

The figures above illustrate the form and are dated; pull current ones fresh rather than
reusing them.

The second version survives being quoted back at you. The first does not.

## Why this matters more for a primer than a visit brief

A visit brief is read by one rep for one meeting. A primer becomes the shared understanding
of an agency and gets copied into decks, forwarded, and quoted months later, long after
whoever wrote it remembers which number came from where. Tier it at the source or it decays
into unattributed assertions.
